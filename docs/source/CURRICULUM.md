LingAfriq: Premium Curriculum Plan (accurate
before native authors)
Prepared for: Tope (Fabian Umole) · Date: 17 October 2026 · Companion to: LINGAFRIQ_ELEVATION_PLAN.md
(§6 curriculum, §17–18 features) and LINGAFRIQ_AI_MODEL_STACK.md (models, costs).
The brief. Build highly accurate, premium curricula for the Stage-1 seven (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Nigerian
Pidgin, Swahili, isiXhosa, isiZulu) and then as many African languages as possible, without native
speakers writing them. Native speakers come later as final validators. Until then, the content must be as
accurate as it can be, and the app must never present an unverified item as certain.
Method. Licences and availability were checked on the live web on 17 Oct 2026 (links inline). Legal points
are a product manager's reading, not legal advice; get counsel to sign off the licence register (§2.4). Costs
and accuracy figures are planning estimates, marked as such, to be replaced by measured numbers from
the pilot.
0. The plan on one page
1. Ground every item in a source, never in the model's memory. Lexemes, tone patterns, grammar
rules and culture claims come from a licence-checked source library (public-domain FSI courses,
commercially licensed dictionaries, CC-BY corpora, official orthography rules). An LLM drafts only
inside that evidence and must cite it claim by claim.
2. Verify with machines that disagree with each other. A second model family cross-checks,
independent MT back-translates, deterministic linters check orthography, tone marks and Bantu
concords, and morphological analysers parse Zulu. Every item gets a confidence score: high scores
publish as Beta, mid scores go to a human queue, low scores are rejected and regenerated.
3. Buy expert spot-checks instead of authors. Paid linguists and academics review 100% of high-risk
items (tone marks with no lexicon match, idioms, culture and history, honorifics, grammar
explanations) and a statistical sample of the rest. That costs a fraction of authoring.
4. Label honestly. Items show Beta, Expert-reviewed or Native-verified; synthetic audio says
"Synthetic voice". Learners can tap "Report a mistake" on every item, and errors flow back into the
pipeline.
5. Rebuild today's broken data through the same pipeline. Pull placeholders from the build, collapse
the three identical A1–C2 copies into one signed content pack, replace the two copy-pasted grammar
lines with a cited Pattern Library, and correct false tone claims by lexicon lookup.
6. Realistic outcome: Beta A1 for all seven languages in about 12 weeks, A2 in about 24 weeks, and B1
Expert-reviewed by about month 9 (all in Beta first). Per language, roughly $4k–15k in content cost to
reach A1–B1 Expert-reviewed, plus one-off licences and tooling (§9). Accuracy targets: §6.

2. Source-grounded authoring: the source library
2.1 Rules of use
Facts versus expression. That ọkọ̀ means "vehicle" with a mid–low tone pattern is a linguistic fact; a
dictionary's wording of the definition and its example sentences are the publisher's expression.
Learner-facing text is written fresh by the pipeline and cites sources as evidence. Copy verbatim
only from public-domain or compatible-licence sources, with attribution. (EU database right can also
protect substantial extraction from a database; counsel should confirm per source.)
Share-alike containment. CC BY-SA material (Wiktionary, UD treebanks, Biblica open Bibles, most
WAXAL subsets) is used as verification evidence, or kept in a clearly separated, attributed CC BY-SA
layer. It is not mixed into LingAfriq's proprietary lexicon text, because that would put the derived
database under CC BY-SA.
Non-commercial means not usable in a paid app without a written licence or waiver. Using such
data only to train or check models is still commercial use of the data; treat it as excluded unless
licensed.
Every source gets a licence register entry: URL, licence, version/date, allowed uses (display,
evidence, training), attribution text, and owner sign-off.

Later waves. Reuse the same template. Each new language needs: 1. an orthography authority; 2. one
licensed or public-domain lexicon; 3. one reference grammar; 4. a CC-BY or PD corpus.
Use Lanfrica to discover sources, and check each one against FSI's and the Peace Corps' language lists. A
language without a lexicon source cannot pass the gates beyond L0 "Taste" until a linguist builds a seed

3. Multi-model verification pipeline
3.1 Stages
1. Language kit (once per language). Allowed character set and Unicode rules (NFC); tone-marking
policy for pedagogy; phone inventory; G2P rules; noun-class and concord tables (Bantu); dialect
standard; a register of honorifics.
2. Lexicon first. Build the frequency list from Leipzig and other CC-BY corpora, then map each frequent
form to a sourced lemma (licensed dictionary first, Wiktionary as a cross-check). Each lexeme stores
tone pattern, IPA from G2P, POS, noun class, plural and sources. A lexeme with two agreeing
sources is "anchored"; with one source, it is "single-source" (held for linguist review if high-
frequency).
3. Grounded generation. Gemini 3.1 Pro (AI stack §3.1/§3.9) receives the can-do target, the allowed
lexicon (anchored lexemes only), the relevant Pattern Library entries and retrieved evidence
passages. It drafts sentences, dialogues, exercises, distractors and explanations as structured JSON
with a citation per claim (source id plus locator). Forbidden: any word outside the allowed lexicon (it
must be requested as a new lexeme), and any culture or grammar claim without a citation.
<!-- page 6 -->
4. Independent cross-check. A different model family (GPT-5 class; a third model as tie-breaker)
receives the item without the generator's reasoning. It translates the item to English, identifies
errors, rates naturalness and register, and checks each citation supports its claim (entailment against
the retrieved passage).
5. Back-translation. Independent MT (Google Cloud Translation; MADLAD-400 for the long tail, AI stack
§3.7) translates the target text back to English, scored by semantic similarity against the intended
meaning. Disagreement flags the item; it doesn't auto-reject it, because MT itself is weak for some
languages.
6. Deterministic linters (§3.3) and analysers (ZulMorph for Zulu; rule-based concord checkers
elsewhere).
7. Tone and diacritic consistency. Every token is matched to a lexicon entry, and its tone marks must
equal the lexicon's for that sense. Where a restoration model exists (NaijaML for Yoruba), it is run as
an independent opinion: the item passes only if the lexicon form, the generator's form and the
restorer agree. Any disagreement goes to a linguist.
8. Confidence scoring and routing (§3.4).
9. Human review where routed (§5), then publishing with a label.

human review. Conjunctive orthography follows the PanSALB rules (Zulu and Xhosa write verbal
complexes as one word).
Pidgin: NLA spelling normalisation table; flags English spellings where an NLA form exists.

3.4 Confidence score and routing
Score = a weighted blend, calibrated on the linguist-reviewed pilot set. Inputs: - lexicon coverage (% of
tokens anchored); - tone agreement (lexicon, generator and restorer); - cross-model agreement and
naturalness; - back-translation similarity; - linter results; - citation entailment; - source tier (licensed
dictionary or PD course > CC-BY corpus > Wiktionary-only).
Route Rule Result
Auto-Beta Score ≥ 0.90, all linters pass, no high-risk tag Publish Beta; enters the linguist sample
Review 0.70–0.90, or any high-risk tag regardless of score Linguist queue; publish as Expert-reviewed when approved
Reject < 0.70 or any hard linter failure Regenerate (max 2 attempts), then park for a human
High-risk tags (never AI-only): - tone-marked tokens without a lexicon match; - idioms and proverbs; -
culture, history and religion claims; - honorifics and respect forms; - grammar explanations; - anything
shown to children.
Thresholds are starting points; tune them so that the measured error rate of Auto-Beta items stays under
the tier target in §6.
4. Pedagogical design
Outcomes: CEFR can-do statements (A1–B1 first), mapped to ACTFL levels for US diaspora and
schools. Each unit targets 2–4 can-dos ("I can greet an elder appropriately in the morning").
Vocabulary: frequency-ranked from CC-BY corpora, adjusted for learner needs (family, food, market,
church/mosque, transport, phone, money). Targets: A1 about 600–800 lexemes, A2 about 1,500, B1
about 2,500–3,000 cumulative.
Spiral syllabus: every pattern and high-frequency lexeme reappears at least 6 times across units in
new contexts (input → controlled practice → speaking → review). FSRS schedules review.
Pattern Library: one entry per real grammatical pattern per language, e.g. Yoruba preverbal ń
(progressive), ti (perfect), máa (future), with examples, contrasts, common learner errors and
citations. Swahili tense markers -na-/-li-/-ta-/-me-; Zulu subject concords by class.
Tone and sound strand: perception and production drills from the lexicon's minimal pairs, plus FSI
tone drills as references (yo, ig, ha); clicks for xh/zu; vowel purity and stress for sw.
Culture strand: short, cited culture cards (greetings, kinship, food, festivals). Proverbs only after
linguist review.
Item schema: the elevation plan §6.5 schema plus provenance.sources[], claims[{text, source_id,
locator}], confidence, risk_tags[], label (beta | expert | native) and audio.kind (tts | human).

8. Rebuilding the existing curriculum data
1. Quarantine. Move the three placeholder bundles and the duplicated expanded bundle out of the

placeholder pattern.
2. Salvage. Extract every real phrase from lingafriq_authentic_curriculum_a1_c2.json (one copy; the
other two identical copies are dropped) and from game_content.json. Each becomes a seed
candidate that goes through the full pipeline: lexicon anchoring, tone check, cross-check. Survivors
keep their history (provenance.source: legacy); failures are logged for the linguist.
3. Grammar. Delete the two generic sentences from all 1,546 lessons and replace them with Pattern
Library entries per language, written from cited grammars (FSI + reference grammars). The claim
linter bans the old strings.
4. Tone claims. Every legacy tonal_note is re-derived from the lexicon's tone pattern. Claims like "High
tone on àárọ̀ marks respect" fail the citation linter and are replaced with correct, cited notes (e.g.,
respect is carried by the honorific pronoun Ẹ). phonetic_guide is regenerated as IPA from G2P plus
the tone pattern, never copied.
5. Dialogues. Regenerate from can-do targets with the anti-repetition linter (no repeated lines; each
turn advances the scene).
6. Structure. Rebuild units against the CEFR map. One signed content pack per language and level,
with content-hash audio filenames and NFC text.
7. UI strings and phrase packs. Run app_*.arb and offline phrase packs through the same
orthography linters and linguist review (e.g., "Ẹ káàbọ̀ sí LingAfriq").
8. Report. Produce a before/after dashboard: items salvaged versus rebuilt, error rate in the sample,
and coverage per CEFR level.
