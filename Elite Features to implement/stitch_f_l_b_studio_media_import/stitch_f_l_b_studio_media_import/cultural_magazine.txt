Across West Africa, in the Sahel, along the coasts, a generation of griots, praise-singers, storytellers and elders is aging out of living tradition. Their successors are choosing other paths. And the communities that once gathered to listen are now scattered across cities and continents, kept company by algorithms that have no patience for a nine-hundred-year genealogy.</p>

      <div class="reader-section-head">What the Archive Cannot Hold</div>

      <p class="reader-text">The problem with recording a griot is not technical. It is contextual. A jeli's performance changes with the audience — when he sees a descendant of a rival clan in the crowd, he navigates. When a patron is present, certain genealogies are emphasized. When there are children, the register shifts. The archive freezes one performance, one night, one version of a truth that by its nature was always plural.</p>
